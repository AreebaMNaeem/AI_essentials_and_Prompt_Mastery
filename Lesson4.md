# 🔎 Week 5: AI for Research and Fact-Checking

> We solve **one real research question from start to finish**, using each tool at the point where the research needs it.

---

## 🎯 Our Case Study

**Research question:**
> *"Does using AI tools weaken students' critical thinking?"*

**What we'll produce:** a one-page research brief with at least 5 verified claims and real sources.

---

## 🔄 The Workflow

```
 1. EXPLORE      →  2. FIND         →  3. READ        →  4. CHECK      →  5. VERIFY     →  6. WRITE
 ChatGPT/Claude     Elicit             NotebookLM        Perplexity       (Manual)         Your brief
 (ideas only)       (real papers)      (summarize)       (current facts)  (open sources)
```

**Golden rule:** AI helps you find and understand. You decide what's true.

---

## 🧠 Stage 1: Explore the Topic (ChatGPT / Claude)

### Apply it to our case study

**Step 1:** Understand the topic.
```
Explain what "critical thinking" means in education and how researchers might study
whether AI tools affect it.
```

**Step 2:** Narrow the question.
```
Suggest 6 specific research questions about how AI tools affect university students'
critical thinking. For each, give 3 keywords I can use to search for papers.
```

**Step 3:** Choose your final question and write down 5 keywords.

## 📖 Stage 3: Read and Summarize (NotebookLM)

### Apply it to our case study

**Step 1:** Create a notebook and upload your 3 papers.

**Step 2:** Get a summary.
```
For each source, summarize the research question, method, sample, how critical thinking
was measured, main findings, and limitations. Use a table.
```

**Step 3:** Compare them.
```
What do these sources agree on? Where do they disagree?
```

**Step 4:** Test it with a question your papers may not cover.
```
What do these sources say about students in Pakistan?
```
A good tool says the sources don't cover it instead of guessing.

**Step 5:** Click 3 citations and read the original passage.

---

## 🌐 Stage 4: Check Current Facts (Perplexity)

### Apply it to our case study

**Step 1:** Ask for usage numbers.
```
According to recent surveys, what share of university students use AI tools for
coursework? List the sources and their dates.
```

**Step 2:** Make it local.
```
What do recent reports say about AI tool use among students in Pakistan or South Asia?
List the sources and their dates.
```

**Step 3:** For each source, check who published it, when, and whether they could benefit from the result (for example, an AI company's own survey).

**Step 4:** Ask for the other side.
```
What do critics say about AI's effect on students' critical thinking? Include sources.
```

## ✅ Stage 5: Verify Everything (Manual Source Check)

**This is the most important step.** No AI tool replaces opening the source yourself.

### The 6-step routine

1. Open the link. Does it load?
2. Use Ctrl+F to find the claim. Does the source really say it?
3. Check the date.
4. Check who wrote it.
5. Find a second independent source.
6. If you can't verify it, leave it out.

## ✍️ Stage 6: Write the Brief

Use **only verified claims**. One page:

1. **Question:** one sentence
2. **What research says:** 3-4 verified findings with sources
3. **Limits and disagreements:** 1-2 points
4. **Current context:** 1-2 verified recent facts
5. **Your conclusion:** in your own words
6. **Sources:** only the ones you opened

**Optional polish:**
```
Improve the clarity of this draft, but do not add any new facts or change my sources.
```
Then re-read it to make sure nothing new slipped in.

## 🧰 Which Tool for Which Job?

| Task | Best tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Brainstorming a research angle | ChatGPT / Claude | Fast and flexible |
| Summarizing PDFs or articles | NotebookLM | Grounded in your own uploads |
| Finding academic papers | Elicit | Searches real literature |
| Checking a current fact or statistic | Perplexity (or similar) | Live search with sources |
| Verifying a specific claim | Manual source check | No AI replaces opening the source |
